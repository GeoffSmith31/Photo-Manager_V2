About Photo Manager

What is Photo Manager?

In the days of film cameras, people often wrote notes on the back of printed photographs. A name, a date, a place, or a few words describing the occasion could preserve valuable information for future generations. These notes can be invaluable to people trying to identify ancestors, relatives, friends, and occasions from the past.

Digital photography has changed all that. We can now take thousands of photographs with very little cost or effort, but many of those images have little or no information to explain who is in them, where they were taken, or what was happening at the time. Even when someone records this information, it is often kept in a separate document, disconnected from the photographs themselves. Over time, the photographs can easily become disassociated from the document, and the information may be lost.

Photo Manager binds the photographs and their associated information together. It provides a structured way to work with photo collections and the information that gives those images meaning, helping preserve that knowledge for future reference.

The aim is simple: to make sure that photographs remain more than just pictures — they retain the stories and information that make them a valuable memory.

Photo Manager lets you record information about photographs and then writes that information directly into the metadata of each photo's file. Because the information is stored within the files themselves, it stays with the photographs when they are copied, moved, or shared, rather than depending on a separate document that can become lost or disconnected from the images.

Many photo viewing applications can display the metadata that is written by Photo Manager.

Supporting Files

Photo Manager stores its application and supporting files in the following locations:

  • Application files: ~/.local/opt/photo-manager/PhotoManager/
  • Uninstaller: ~/.local/opt/photo-manager/uninstall.sh
  • Application launcher: ~/.local/share/applications/photo-manager.desktop
  • Configuration settings: ~/.config/photo_manager/settings.json
  • Application log: ~/.local/share/photo_manager/logs/photo_manager.log

The release archive also contains README-UBUNTU.txt, which provides installation information. It is not installed with the application.

The ~ represents your home directory. On a typical installation, these paths begin with /home/ followed by your username.
